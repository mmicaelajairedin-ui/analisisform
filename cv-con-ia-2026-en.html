How to Make a Resume with AI in 2026.

6 min read · May 2026
Back to blog

In 2026, building a resume "by hand" in Word no longer makes much sense. AI does it better, faster, and tailored to the job. But not every tool gives the same result, and there are very common mistakes that ruin the output.

This guide is what I recommend after generating 800+ resumes with AI over the past year, reading them from the recruiter's side of the desk.

Why AI changes the resume game

Three years ago, a good resume was a matter of a pretty template plus well-written text. Today three things matter more:

  1. Tailoring to the job. A single resume won't work for 5 different openings. AI can rewrite your bullets to align with each posting in seconds.
  2. ATS optimization. A large share of resumes at mid-size and large companies pass through automated filtering systems before reaching a human. AI is good at identifying the job's keywords and making sure they show up in your resume.
  3. Consistency with LinkedIn and cover letter. Resume + LinkedIn + cover letter should tell the same story. AI is perfect for keeping that consistency.

The real shift: before, the bottleneck was writing well. Now it's knowing what to ask the AI for.

What AI does well — and what it doesn't

It does well:

  • Rewriting boring bullets into high-impact ones. "Responsible for the sales team" → "Led a team of 12, growing sales +34% in 18 months." This is where it adds the most value.
  • Suggesting keywords from the job. If you paste in the job description, it identifies terms your resume should include.
  • Catching inconsistencies. Date errors, contradictions, redundancies.
  • Adapting tone to the industry (formal for banking, casual for a startup).

It does NOT do well (yet):

  • Inventing achievements you didn't give it. If you don't know how many people you led, the AI doesn't either. You have to supply the raw data.
  • Knowing the specific culture of your target company. A general AI doesn't know that Company X values certain terms over others. That's what an experienced coach brings.
  • Telling you which experiences to highlight and which to minimize. That takes editorial judgment — AI tends to give everything similar weight.
  • Designing it visually. Unless you use a tool with a template, you're still pasting the text into Word.

Step by step: how to build your resume with AI the right way

Whether you use ChatGPT, Claude or a specialized tool, this is the flow that works:

1. Gather your raw data first

Before you open anything, write down in a document:

  • Each job with: company, title, dates, location, and 3-5 actions you took.
  • For each action, try to add numbers: how many people, how much money, how much time, what percentage.
  • The professional target you're aiming for (not generic — specific).
  • If you have the target job description, paste that in too.

This is 80% of the work. AI is good at rewriting, but not at inventing.

2. Ask for a rewrite with judgment

A good prompt for Claude or ChatGPT:

"Here's my experience [paste]. Rewrite each bullet with a past-tense action verb, concrete numbers when available, and relevant keywords for a [target role]. Keep a maximum of 5 bullets per role. No empty adjectives like 'passionate' or 'responsible for'."

3. Validate against the ATS

If you use ChatGPT/Claude, paste the result into a tool like Resume Worded or use our Pack Express, which includes ATS analysis. It gives you a score and the keywords you're missing.

4. Design the format

Good text + a clean design = a resume that passes filters and reads well. Your options:

  • Word/Pages with a template: free, but it'll take you 1-2 hours.
  • Canva or Resume.io: fast, but the elegant formats sometimes fail in an ATS.
  • Specialized tool: delivers the formatted PDF directly.

5. Generate the cover letter and review LinkedIn

Your resume doesn't travel alone. The cover letter should match your resume (same keywords, same tone), and so should your LinkedIn. If the three aren't consistent, the recruiter notices.

Mistakes I see most often

  1. Asking the AI to "make a resume" without giving it data. It ends up inventing. Bad.
  2. Not validating against an ATS. The resume looks gorgeous but bounces off the automated filter.
  3. Sending the same resume to every opening. Tailoring per role adds 5 minutes and triples your responses.
  4. Ignoring the cover letter. In many hiring processes it still tips the scales.
  5. Forgetting LinkedIn. Recruiters check your LinkedIn before your resume. If your headline says "Seeking opportunities," you've lost.
