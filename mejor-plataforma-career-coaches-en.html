Why are career coaches leaving Excel and Drive?

Career coaching has grown a lot since 2020. More professionals in transition, more demand, more coaches entering the field. But most still use the same generic tools they used five years ago: Excel, Google Drive, Calendly, ChatGPT.

The problem isn't that any one of them works badly on its own. The problem is the friction between them:

  • A client asks to see their progress → you have nothing visual to show, you just say "we're around week 2"
  • You need to generate a diagnostic report → you copy the intake form out of Drive, paste it into ChatGPT, tweak the prompt, copy the answer, paste it into Word, email it
  • You want to know how many active clients you have this month → you open Excel, count rows, add up payments, adjust by hand
  • A client pays you through Stripe → you log it manually in Excel so you don't lose track
A concrete number: a coach with 10 active clients spends between 6 and 10 hours a week on admin alone when they use separate generic tools. With a purpose-built platform, that drops to 1-2 hours a week. That's 8 extra hours a month for real sessions or your own life.

What does a career coaching platform need in 2026?

After guiding 200+ professionals and building Pathway, I landed on the 10 features a tool has to have to be useful for serious career coaching:

1. A client CRM with clear statuses

Not just name and email. Where they are in the process (week 1 to 6), outstanding payments, last contact, private coach notes, photo.

2. A client portal open 24/7

Your client should see their own progress without having to message you every time. Resume in progress, cover letter, LinkedIn optimization, job suggestions, upcoming sessions. The platform works for them too — not just for you.

3. AI built specifically for career coaching

Generic ChatGPT doesn't know your client's context. A purpose-built platform uses the intake form + work history + industry + location to generate reports and suggestions that are actually personalized. The gap between "an AI answer" and "an AI answer with context" is enormous in quality — and generic coaching platforms don't have this at all.

4. A resume builder with ATS optimization

ATS-friendly templates (so they pass recruiters' automated filters), editable by the client under your supervision, exportable to PDF with the colors intact. This is the single tool your clients need most — and the one no generic coaching platform ships.

5. Automated job suggestions

The platform suggests roles to your client filtered by industry, location and level — with direct links to Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs, Glassdoor and other portals. You don't have to email links by hand.

6. Payments integrated with Stripe

The client pays, the platform records it. You don't reconcile Excel against the Stripe dashboard — and you keep 100% of what you charge, with 0% commission on your own clients.

7. Calendar and scheduling

Calendly embedded or a built-in system. Automatic reminders to the client. Sessions logged in the portal.

8. Communication templates

Welcome emails, reminders, end-of-week check-ins, testimonial requests. Ready to send by WhatsApp, email, or copy and paste (they work in English and Spanish).

9. A white-label portal with your own brand

Your client's portal should use your logo and your color, not the platform's. Your client should feel they're in your digital practice, not on a third-party app.

10. A directory that brings you new clients

This is the most underrated feature of all, and the one that separates Pathway from everything else. Every other platform helps you manage clients you already have. Pathway also helps you get new ones: coaches appear in a public directory and marketplace where people looking for a career coach can find and book them.

The feature nobody else has: it brings you clients

How to choose: key questions before you switch

  1. How many active clients do you manage? With 1-3, Excel is fine. At 4+, a purpose-built platform starts to pay off.
  2. How much time goes to admin vs. real coaching? If it's more than 30%, there's room to automate.
